## 2. Hero photography / video

### Shot type
**Aerial wide establishing shot** — drone, looking diagonally downward at the full plant facility. Not straight-down satellite view. A ¾-angle that captures the entire processing area, storage spheres, truck access roads, and surrounding land in one frame.

### Camera height
80–120 metres AGL (above ground level). High enough to show the full site layout, low enough that individual structures remain recognisable.

### Lens recommendation
- **Full-frame camera:** 24–35mm equivalent. Enough width for the full site, tight enough to avoid distortion at the edges.
- **Drone:** DJI Mavic 3 or equivalent, 24mm equivalent, sensor native. No fisheye. No ultra-wide distortion.
- **If ground-level hero alternative:** 50mm equivalent from 10–15m distance, 2–3m camera height. Still wide enough for context, tight enough for detail.

### Composition
- Rule of thirds: plant occupies the lower third to lower half. Sky occupies the upper third to upper half (but sky should not dominate — 30–40% max).
- Leading lines: access roads, pipeline paths, or row structures lead the eye from foreground into the plant.
- Symmetry or structured asymmetry: the plant's layout determines the composition. If the plant is geometrically laid out, shoot square-on. If it follows a natural contour, shoot at ¾-angle.
- No dead centre unless the plant is perfectly symmetrical (power-plant style).

### Subject placement
- The core processing area (digesters, storage spheres, purification columns) sits in the lower-left or lower-right third.
- Sky occupies the upper third but should contain interesting cloud texture — not flat blue, not dramatic sunset.
- No people visible. The hero establishes the infrastructure, not the workforce.

### Motion (video hero)
- **Camera movement:** Slow push-in or slow dolly forward. 10–15mm movement over 12 seconds. Not a zoom — a physical camera move.
- **Speed:** 5mm/s approximately. The movement should be almost imperceptible on first glance. The viewer should only notice after 4–5 seconds that the camera is moving.
- **Stabilisation:** Gimbal-locked. No handheld wobble. No micro-jitters.
- **Framing:** Start slightly wider than the ideal composition, end at the ideal composition.
- **If no drone video is possible:** A slowly rotating gimbal pan from a fixed ground position, 30° arc over 15 seconds.

### Video duration
12–20 seconds before loop.

### Loop behaviour
**Hard cut to start frame.** No crossfade, no reverse-play. The video should be cut so that the first and last frames are close enough that the hard cut is not jarring — camera at same position, similar cloud coverage. If the loop cut is visible, it's acceptable as long as it's not disruptive. A seamless loop is not required.

### Crop rules
- 16:9 native. On ultra-wide monitors (21:9), crop the top and bottom equally — never crop the sides.
- The core subject (plant structures) must remain visible in all crops down to 16:9.
- On mobile (9:16 or similar), crop to the most visually dense region — typically the lower half showing the processing equipment. Sky is sacrificed.

### Colour grading
- **White balance:** 5600K (neutral daylight). No warming. No cooling beyond neutral.
- **Contrast:** Medium-high. Deep blacks, clean whites. S-curve.
- **Saturation:** −15% from natural. Desaturated enough to feel industrial, not enough to look grey.
- **Shadow treatment:** Crushed slightly. Shadows fall to near-black (navy-toned black: `oklch(0.05 0.005 260)`), not pure black.
- **Highlight treatment:** Rolled off gently. No blown-out skies. Keep highlight detail in white/grey cloud cover.
- **Green handling:** Greens are shifted toward teal-blue. Desaturate by −30%. No lush green grass, no warm green foliage. Muddy olive or blue-green tones.
- **Sky treatment:** Pull cyan toward blue. Reduce magenta. A slightly desaturated steel-grey or pale blue sky.
- **Metal treatment:** Silver/stainless steel should read as cool silver, not warm. Painted structures (navy, white) should have their colour preserved but with reduced saturation.
- **Grade reference:** Look like an Ørsted press photo shot on a slightly overcast day with a polarising filter.

### Weather / lighting
- Preferred: overcast or lightly overcast. Soft, even light. No harsh shadows. No direct sun.
- Acceptable: early morning golden hour only if it's subtle (warmth kept below +300K from neutral).
- Forbidden: midday harsh sun (hard shadows, blown highlights). Sunset/sunrise with dramatic orange sky. Rain. Fog (unless very light and even). Snow.
- The goal is "industrial lighting" — the kind of even, shadowless light that makes factory floors look clean and measurable.

### Things to avoid
- People in frame. No workers walking, no hard hats, no silhouettes.
- Birds. Animals. Wildlife.
- Flags. Decorative banners. Signage for other companies.
- Construction equipment (cranes, scaffolding, temporary structures) — if the plant is still being built, don't show that in the hero.
- Power lines, telecom towers, non-company infrastructure in the foreground.
- Reflections in water puddles.
- Lens flare.
- Vignette.
- HDR effect.
- Any colour that competes with navy and white (red, orange, yellow objects).

## 3. Photography direction

### Required shots (by priority)

**Priority 1 — Infrastructure scale**
- Wide drone shot of the full plant (primary hero candidate)
- Mid-altitude drone shot focused on the biogas storage spheres or digesters
- Ground-level wide shot of the plant exterior, 50mm, showing facade + sky

**Priority 2 — Processing equipment**
- Purification columns / scrubbing towers, 50–85mm, from ground level looking slightly up
- Pipe networks — tight composition, 85mm, diagonal lines filling the frame
- Control valves and pressure gauges, 100mm macro, shallow depth of field
- Digester vessels, exterior, wide enough to show full height

**Priority 3 — Logistics & infrastructure**
- Bio-CNG cascades (storage cylinders for transport), side-on, 50mm
- Truck loading bay, empty or with a single truck, 35mm
- Fleet vehicle (truck) — ¾ front angle, neutral background, 50mm
- Fertiliser storage warehouse or bagged product on pallets, 35mm

**Priority 4 — Laboratory & quality**
- Lab technician at work (from behind or side, no face), 85mm
- Lab equipment close-up — titration, gas chromatography, 100mm macro
- Sample jars, labelled, on a clean white surface, top-down, 50mm

**Priority 5 — Agricultural context (lowest priority)**
- Farmland adjacent to or supplied by the plant, wide, overcast light
- Fertiliser application on field, 50mm — no farmer visible, just the spreading equipment
- Biomass feedstock storage or collection point, 35mm

### Preferred framing
- **Exteriors:** Subject positioned in lower third with negative space above. If a structure is tall (scrubber column), allow headroom so it doesn't touch the top edge.
- **Interiors:** Clean backgrounds. No clutter. No cables on the floor. No personal items (water bottles, jackets, phones).
- **Details:** Fill the frame. A valve, a gauge, a weld seam — no context needed around it.
- **People (if unavoidable):** Three-quarter shot from behind or side profile. Never looking at camera. Operator in uniform (navy or white). Hard hat on. Gloves. No jewellery, no visible phone, no branded clothing beyond company logo.

### Background treatment
- Clean. Empty. No competing elements.
- If indoors, walls should be white or light grey. If painted, navy or steel grey.
- If outdoors, no vehicles not owned by Shaw AgriVolt. No other industrial sites visible.
- Depth of field: use shallow DoF for detail shots (gauges, valves, lab equipment) to isolate subject from background. Use deep DoF for scale shots (wide exterior, drone) to keep everything sharp.

### Negative space
- Generous negative space on the side where text will sit. For the hero, this means columns 1–6 (left) should have minimal visual competition — the subject in that area should be simple (sky, clean wall, open ground).
- For section 7 (case study, 60/40 split), the text side (left) should carry minimal photographic weight — keep that area as clean sky, smooth wall, or uniform surface.
- When shooting, frame wider than necessary. Leave 20% extra headroom and side room for cropping and text placement.

### Human presence
- **Preference:** No humans visible. The infrastructure is the subject.
- **When needed (lab, operations):** Maximum one person per frame. In uniform. No eye contact with camera. Hands visible and doing something (reading a gauge, adjusting equipment, writing a log).
- **Never:** Smiling. Gesturing. Pointing at the camera. Standing with arms crossed. Holding a hard hat at the hip. Group photos.
- **If the person's face is recognisable, they must have signed a release.** Preferred: shoot from behind or at an angle where features are not identifiable.

### Machinery emphasis
- Machines are clean. Wiped down before shooting. No oil stains on the floor. No rust. No peeling paint.
- Labels and nameplates visible and legible.
- Pipes colour-coded (if already painted) — these should match or complement the navy/white palette. If they are bright yellow or red, crop them out or desaturate.
- Storage spheres: shoot from a low angle to emphasise height. Clean the surface — no bird droppings, no dust streaks.

### Scale references
- Include a known-size object in wide shots where possible: a standard shipping container, a truck, a person (small in frame). This lets the viewer intuitively understand the plant scale.
- For macro/detail shots, no scale reference needed — the texture and precision are the subject.

## 5. Image composition rules

### Left vs right balance
- Most content sections are left-aligned text (following the design DNA). Photography sits on the right side or behind the content.
- Shoot frames with the main subject in the right two-thirds of the frame, leaving the left third relatively clean.
- If the composition must be mirrored, provide both left-subject and right-subject versions.

### Rule of thirds
- Always apply. Horizon at upper third or lower third — never centre.
- Main subject at an intersection point (lower-left or lower-right preferred).
- Only break for macro detail shots and symmetrical architectural shots.

### Leading lines
- Pipes, roads, rail tracks, row structures — use these to lead into the frame toward the main subject.
- Diagonal lines are preferred over horizontal or vertical.
- Lines should not exit the frame at corners — enter from the left/right edge midpoint and terminate at the subject.

### Horizon placement
- **Lower third:** When sky is interesting (clouds, texture). Better for most exterior shots — emphasises scale.
- **Upper third:** When foreground/infrastructure is the subject. Emphasises equipment detail.
- **Never centre:** Creates a flat, amateur composition.

### Depth
- Three layers preferred: foreground element (out of focus or partial), midground (subject), background (context/sky).
- Foreground examples: a pipe edge, a structural beam, a piece of machinery slightly OOF to frame the shot.
- Flat compositions (no depth) only for product shots against clean backgrounds.

### Cropping rules
- Never crop through joints or connection points (pipes should end at frame edge or continue, not be cut mid-pipe).
- Never crop through a person's head or hands.
- Leave 10–15% margin around the intended final crop — allows for layout adjustments.
- Maintain 16:9 aspect ratio for all video. 4:3 or 3:2 for stills (can be cropped to 16:9 later).

### Empty space
- Empty space (sky, clean wall, ground) is an intentional design element. Shoot so that empty space occupies at least 30% of the frame in hero and section-establishing shots.
- This space will hold typography and UI elements. The photo is the background; the text is the foreground.

### Overlay restrictions
- **No gradient overlays** on any photograph. Zero transparency gradients (black-to-clear, white-to-clear).
- **No colour overlays.** No tinting the image to match the brand palette. The image is full-colour, natural, cool-graded.
- **No text on the image** except the hero headline, which sits on the clean portion of the image (not overlapping the subject).
- **No logos overlaid on photography** except the SATAT badge in the hero (top-left, small, no larger than 48×48px).

## 6. Colour grading

### Master grade
All photography receives the same master grade for consistency across the site.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Colour space | Rec. 709 (SDR). No HDR on the web. |
| White balance | 5600K ± 200K |
| Contrast | +15 (medium-high) |
| Highlights | −5 (roll off) |
| Shadows | −10 (crush slightly) |
| Whites | +5 |
| Blacks | −8 |
| Saturation | −15 |
| Vibrance | −10 |
| Sharpening | +20 (moderate) |

### Per-element adjustments

| Element | Adjustment |
|---------|------------|
| **Sky** | Blues shifted −10° toward cyan. Luminance −5. No purple/magenta cast. |
| **Greens (vegetation)** | Hue shifted +20° toward olive. Saturation −40. Luminance −10. |
| **Greens (painted — brand green)** | Restore to brand `oklch(0.45 0.12 160)`. Preserve saturation. |
| **Navy (painted structures)** | Restore to brand `oklch(0.35 0.04 260)`. Preserve. |
| **Whites (buildings, tanks)** | Neutralise. Warm tint removed. Should read as cool white. |
| **Metals (silver, steel)** | Cool tint maintained. Keep highlights crisp. |
| **Skin tones** | Desaturate −20. Keep neutral — not warm, not green. |
| **Concrete / pavement** | Cool grey. Reduce yellow. |
| **Orange / red (unavoidable)** | Desaturate −60. Luminance −15. These colours should recede. |

### Black levels
- Blacks are not pure `#000000`. They should measure approximately `oklch(0.05 0.005 260)` — a navy-tinted near-black.
- This matches the brand's near-black navy footer background.
- Crushed shadow detail is acceptable — the priority is a solid, clean black with colour tone.

### Green handling — critical
Natural greens (grass, trees, crops) must be desaturated and shifted toward olive/teal. This is a deliberate choice to avoid "green company" cliché. The brand green is an accent used only in the UI and certification marks — not in the photography. If the photography contains bright green vegetation, it will conflict with the brand green. Desaturate aggressively.

## 7. Lighting direction

### Natural light — exteriors
| Condition | Suitability | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Overcast, bright | **Preferred** | Soft, even light. No hard shadows. Good for all exteriors. |
| Overcast, dark | Acceptable | May require exposure compensation. Watch for noise. |
| Early morning (civil twilight) | Acceptable | Low contrast, soft. Warmth must be corrected in grade. |
| Golden hour | **Not preferred** | Too warm. Hard shadows. Conflicts with cool brand palette. |
| Midday, clear | **Avoid** | Harsh shadows, blown highlights. Unforgiving on metal. |
| Blue hour | Acceptable for hero only | Moody, dramatic. Use only if the plant has good artificial lighting. |
| Rain / fog / snow | **Avoid** | Reduces visibility of infrastructure. |

### Industrial light — interiors / process areas
- **Preferred:** Overhead fluorescent or LED. Clean, white (4000–5000K). Even distribution.
- **Acceptable:** Mixed lighting (windows + artificial). Correct white balance to 4500K.
- **Avoid:** Sodium vapour lamps (orange). Single-point tungsten (harsh shadows). Unlit areas.
- **Before shooting:** Turn on all available lights. Remove any coloured gel filters from fixtures. Replace any flickering tubes.

### Equipment considerations
- No on-camera flash. No speedlight. The light should look ambient, not lit.
- For interior detail shots: use a softbox or LED panel from above or side at 45°. Key light at 4500K, fill at 4800K. Ratio 2:1.
- No rim lighting, no dramatic side lighting. Flat, clinical, even.

### Reflections
- **Metal surfaces:** Avoid direct reflection of the photographer or camera. Shoot at an angle. Use a polarising filter for exteriors to cut glare.
- **Glass (control room windows, gauge faces):** Angle the camera to avoid reflections. If unavoidable, shoot through a circular polariser.
- **Puddles / water:** Avoid. If present, drain or wait for them to dry before shooting exteriors.
- **Fertiliser granules / product shots:** polariser to reduce glare from shiny surfaces.

## 8. Iconography

2px consistent stroke on all icons. No variation.

### Corner style
Rounded caps. Rounded joins. 0.5px corner radius on all corners.

### Grid
All icons on a 24×24px canvas. Content within a 20×20px live area (2px padding inside the canvas).

## 9. Data visualisation style

### KPI treatment
- KPI numbers use the display typeface, not a chart or graphic.
- The number is the visual. No bar charts, no gauges, no speedometer graphics.
- Thin top border line (2px, 40px wide, navy) above each number is the only graphic element.

### Charts (if needed in Section 2 — The Shift)
- **Style:** Minimal. No gridlines. No background fill. No legend (label directly on or beside the data).
- **Colour:** Single colour — navy (`oklch(0.35 0.04 260)`) for the data stroke or fill. White or off-white background.
- **Type:** Bar chart or simple line chart. No pie charts, no donuts, no radar, no bubble charts.
- **Bar width:** 8px for vertical bars. No rounded bars. Flat caps.
- **Line weight:** 2px for line charts.
- **Axis labels:** 11px, uppercase, caption style. Navy.
- **Data labels:** Directly on bars or at line points. 12px, display or body. Muted colour.
- **Annotations:** Small caption below or beside. Never inside the chart area.
- **Interaction:** None static. No animation beyond fade-in on scroll.

### Tables (not expected on homepage — included for completeness)
- See design-dna.md Section 7 (Component philosophy > Tables).
- If a spec table appears on the homepage, it follows the same style: no alternating row colours, clean borders, right-aligned numbers, display face for numeric data.

### Technical diagrams
- If a process flow diagram is needed (Section 3 alternative to step cards), it must be:
  - 2-colour: navy foreground lines, off-white or white background
  - 2px line weight
  - No 3D perspective — flat orthographic
  - Labels in body typeface, 12px, uppercase
  - Arrows: 2px, 45° arrowhead
  - No shading, no gradients, no drop shadows

### Labels
- Chart labels, diagram labels, and spec labels all follow the same style:
  - Body typeface (not display)
  - 11–12px
  - 500 weight
  - 0.04em letter-spacing
  - Uppercase (single-line labels) or sentence case (multi-line)
  - Navy (`--color-muted`) default

### Annotation style
- Single-line annotations below data: 12px, body typeface, italic recommended, `--color-muted`.
- Call-out annotations on diagrams: 12px, body, connected by a 1px navy line to the referenced element.
- No circled numbers, no asterisks, no footnote markers.

## 10. Texture & background system

### Paper / surface textures
- **No paper textures.** No parchment, no grain, no fibre textures anywhere.
- Surfaces are flat, clean, digital. Design DNA specifies `--color-bg` (white) and `--color-surface` (off-white). These are solid fills, not textured.

### Metal textures
- Photography supplies the only metal textures on the site. No generated brushed-metal backgrounds.
- If a subtle metallic background is needed (unlikely on this project), use a very low-opacity photograph of a clean metal surface, not a Photoshop filter.

### Concrete textures
- If used (for industrial feel), the concrete must be smooth, clean, and consistent — polished concrete, not rough-pour. Colour: warm grey (`oklch(0.6 0.01 80)`) or cool grey (`oklch(0.6 0.01 260)`). Use only in dedicated background panels, never behind text at body size.

### Grid patterns
- Optional: a very subtle structural grid — 1px lines, `--color-border` at 30% opacity, 40×40px grid cells.
- Use only as a repeating background pattern on the process section (Section 3) to reinforce the engineering theme.
- The grid must be barely perceptible — it is texture, not a pattern.

### Noise
- **No grain / noise overlays.** Not on photography, not on UI, not on backgrounds.
- If photography has natural noise from low-light conditions, it must be denoised to clean.

### Patterns
- **No repeating geometric patterns** (chevrons, dots, stripes, herringbone, hexagons).
- **No brand patterns.** The logo is not die-cut into a repeating background.
- The only pattern-like element is the optional grid texture described above.

### Divider usage
- Dividers between sections: 1px solid `--color-border`, 40–80px width, centred, with generous vertical whitespace (equal to `--space-lg` above and below). Act as a subtle pause between narrative sections.
- Dividers are thin, short, and centred. Not full-width. Not dashed. Not dotted.
- Where sections change background colour (white → off-white → white), no divider is needed — the colour shift is the divider.

### Background colour transitions
- Section backgrounds alternate: white → off-white → white → off-white → ... This creates rhythm without patterns or textures.
- No gradient transitions between background colours. Hard edge at the section boundary.
- Exception: the CTA section (navy background) may have a subtle top fade if the transition from white is too harsh — maximum 40px gradient.

## 11. Motion art direction

### Camera movement (video)
- **Hero:** Slow push-in, max 15s duration, looped with hard cut. Movement must be gimbal-stabilised, perfectly linear. No easing on the camera move — constant speed.
- **If no video:** Static photo with no parallax and no Ken Burns effect. The hero photograph is completely static.

### Scroll feeling
- The page should feel solid and grounded. No parallax layers. No sticky elements (except the nav). No scroll jacking.
- The only scroll-related motion is content fading in as sections enter the viewport.
- The page should feel like a printed magazine in scroll form — deliberate, calm, weighted.

### Transition style
- **Between sections:** No transition. The next section appears on scroll with no dissolve, no slide, no wipe. Crossfade between background colours if needed, maximum 300ms, `ease-out`.
- **Content within a section:** Fade up + translateY(24px→0). 600ms. Easing: `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`. This is the only entrance animation.
- **Stagger:** Elements within a section fade in sequence: overline first, then headline, then body, then CTA. 100ms between each.
- **Cards in a grid:** Fade up in sequence left-to-right (desktop), top-to-bottom (mobile). 150ms stagger.

### Image reveal
- **Photography:** No reveal animation. The image is either static (always visible) or fades up with the section content.
- **No diagonal reveals.** No circular reveals. No clip-path animations on images.
- **No lazy-load blur-up.** Images load crisp or not at all.

### Video behaviour
- **Hero video:** Plays on load, muted, autoplay, loop. No play/pause controls visible (keyboard-operable pause button required for accessibility — hidden by default, visible on keyboard focus to the hero section).
- **No inline video elsewhere** on the homepage. Video appears only in the hero.
- **Loading:** Video shows a static poster frame (the first frame, or a specifically selected key frame) while the video loads. The poster frame is identical in composition to the video's first frame.

### Reduced motion
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → disable all entrance animations. Content is fully visible on load.
- Hero video still plays (content, not motion), but the slow push-in is locked to the first frame (static).
- No count-up animation for KPIs. Show the final number immediately.

### Preferred aspect ratios

| Use case | Aspect ratio | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|

### Compression rules
- **Photography:** JPEG quality 85–90 for hero/featured images. 75–80 for secondary images. No visible artifacts at 100% zoom.
- **Hero video:** H.264 or H.265 (HEVC). Bitrate: 15–20 Mbps for 4K. 8–12 Mbps for 1080p. Audio: AAC, 128kbps, stereo (if audio present — hero is muted on web). Two variants: 4K for desktop, 1080p for mobile.
- **Never use:** GIF for anything. PNG for photography. BMP. TIFF on the web. WEBP for lossless photography (use AVIF instead).

### Retina support
- All raster images must be available at 2x resolution for Retina/HiDPI displays.
- The HTML page serves 1x by default and uses `srcset` to serve 2x at larger breakpoints.
- 1x: 1920px wide. 2x: 3840px wide.

### Web optimisation formats
| Image type | Format | Fallback |
|------------|--------|----------|
| Photography | AVIF | WebP → JPEG |
| Graphics / logos | SVG | — |
| Screenshots (not used) | — | — |
| Certification badges | SVG preferred | PNG 2x |

### AVIF / WebP recommendations
- **AVIF:** Enable. Set quality to 40–50 for photography (excellent compression, visually lossless). Chroma subsampling: 4:2:0.
- **WebP:** Enable as fallback for browsers that don't support AVIF. Quality 80.
- **JPEG:** Last resort fallback. Quality 85.
- Serve via `<picture>` element with AVIF → WebP → JPEG order.

## 14. AI generation rules

> AI-generated imagery should never replace original photography. Use only as a last resort when a required shot is impossible to capture (plant not yet built, equipment not installed, access denied).

### Required realism level
- Photorealistic. The image must be indistinguishable from a photograph taken with a full-frame camera.
- The AI image must pass the "print test" — if printed at A3 size and placed next to a real photograph, an expert should not be able to identify which is AI-generated.
- No painterly style. No illustrative style. No "cinematic" excess (dramatic lighting, lens flare, perfect skies).

### Camera metadata style
- Simulate a Sony A7R IV or Canon R5 sensor. Full-frame, 35mm equivalent.
- ISO 100–400. Aperture f/5.6–f/11 (deep depth of field for wide shots).
- Shutter speed: 1/125s minimum for stills. 1/50s for video (cinematic motion blur).
- No grain. No noise.

### Lens simulation
- Wide shots: 24mm or 35mm prime. Minimal distortion.
- Detail shots: 85mm or 100mm macro. Natural bokeh (not artificial blur).
- No fisheye. No tilt-shift. No anamorphic (unless full-width hero video).

### Materials
- Metal: clean stainless steel, cool silver, brushed finish. No rust, no patina.
- Concrete: smooth-pour, clean, consistent. No cracks, no stains, no formwork marks.
- Paint: navy (`oklch(0.35 0.04 260)`), white, light grey. No fading, no chipping.
- Glass: clean, transparent. No reflections of people or equipment.
- Plastic/PVC (pipes): grey or black. Clean. New condition.
- Ground: paved or gravel, clean and even. No mud, no standing water.

### Scale accuracy
- All objects must be correctly scaled relative to each other. A biogas storage sphere must be the correct size relative to a truck. A person must be the correct size relative to a digester.
- Include a scale reference (truck, person, door) in every wide shot to anchor scale.
- No forced perspective tricks that alter perceived size.
- If the model struggles with scale, generate individual elements and composite them rather than generating one full scene.

### Things AI must never generate
- **People.** Never generate a human face, body, or silhouette. If a person is needed in frame, use a real photograph composited into the scene or shoot a real person against green screen and insert them.
- **Text.** Never generate text on signs, labels, or equipment. AI-generated text is always illegible and destroys credibility. Add real text/logos in post-production.
- **Flags, banners, logos.** No company logos or SATAT badges generated by AI. These must be added as vector overlays.
- **Plants and vegetation** that look artificial or "too perfect." AI vegetation often has a glossy, plastic quality. If vegetation is in frame, desaturate in post (see colour grading rules) to reduce the uncanny look.
- **Sun, sky, clouds** that look painted. AI skies are frequently overcooked. Use a real sky plate and composite if the generated sky is not photorealistic.
- **Reflections in metal/glass** that show non-existent objects (a palm tree in an industrial plant, an impossible building). Check all reflections and composite if wrong.
- **Gauges / dials / screens** with readable numbers. All AI-generated instrument panels contain garbled text. Cover these with real gauge photography or blur them out.
- **Fences, railings, grates** that merge into adjacent objects. AI frequently fuses thin structures. Check all edges and mask/fix in post.
- **Double doors, three-prong plugs, six-fingered hands** — the usual AI artifacts. Inspect at 200% zoom before approving.

### Generation prompt structure
When generating, use this prompt template:

```
Photorealistic industrial photograph. [Plant type] facility exterior on a bright overcast day. 
Camera: full-frame, 35mm, f/8, 1/125s, ISO 100. 
Composition: [wide establishing / mid / close-up] of [subject: storage spheres, processing columns, pipe network, truck loading bay]. 
Subject positioned in lower-right third. Clean grey sky in upper third. 
Lighting: soft, even, neutral overcast. No shadows. 
Colour palette: cool grey, navy blue, white, stainless steel. 
No people. No logos. No text. No lens flare. No vignette. 
Highest detail on [specific element: weld seams, bolt patterns, valve wheels].
```

After generation:
1. Inspect at 200% for AI artifacts.
2. Fix garbled text, fused objects, impossible reflections in post.
3. Colour grade to match the guide (cool, desaturated, navy blacks).
4. Composite any logos, badges, or text as vector overlays.

## 15. Visual anti-patterns

### Photography / imagery — complete forbidden list

| Anti-pattern | Why it's forbidden |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Sunset/sunrise golden hour lighting | Too warm. Conflicts with cool brand palette. Reads as "green energy brochure." |
| Sun flare / lens flare | Amateur. Distracting. Inconsistent across the site. |
| HDR or tone-mapped photography | Artificial. Makes metal look plastic. |
| High-contrast black & white photography | Loses the brand navy. Eliminates the industrial detail. |
| Selective colour (one item coloured, rest B&W) | 2005 graphic design trend. Cheapens the brand. |
| Warm skin tones | Only applies if people are visible. Correct white balance. |
| People smiling or looking at camera | Makes it a "happy worker" stock photo. Destroys industrial credibility. |
| Children / families / community groups | NGO brochure visual. Not our audience. |
| Animals / birds / wildlife | Irrelevant. Distracts from infrastructure. |
| Hand-drawn illustrations of any kind | Banned by Design DNA. Never. |
| 3D renders of plants that don't exist yet | Dishonest. If the plant isn't built, don't show a fake version. |
| Aerial shots that include non-company facilities adjacent | Creates confusion about what the company owns. |
| Stock photography of generic industrial equipment | If it's not Shaw AgriVolt's equipment, don't use it. |
| Blurry / out-of-focus photography used as background | Intentional blur reads as a cheap way to hide low quality. |
| Photo collages / multiple images stitched together | One strong image beats three mediocre ones. |
| Mirrored / flipped photography | Never flip a photo horizontally. Equipment labels will be backwards. |
| Watermarks / credits / copyright notices on images | Should be removed before delivery. |
| QR codes in images | Consumer-grade. Not enterprise B2B. |
| Photographs of screens / monitors / tablets | Depicts a screen within a screen. Removes the viewer from reality. |

### Icon / graphic — complete forbidden list

| Anti-pattern | Why it's forbidden |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Leaf / tree / plant icons | Green-energy cliché. Our brand is engineering, not nature. |
| Recycling arrows | Makes the company look like a waste management service, not an energy producer. |
| Globe / earth icons | Too broad. Every sustainability company uses them. |
| Hand-drawn icons | Inconsistent with the precise industrial aesthetic. |
| Filled icons | Only outlined icons are permitted (2px stroke). |
| Gradient icons | Only solid-colour icons. |
| Multi-colour icons | Only single-colour icons. |
| Emoji used as icons | Unprofessional. Not a design choice. |
| Animated icons | Motion on icons is unnecessary and distracting. |
| Social media icons for Instagram, Facebook, TikTok | Not appropriate for enterprise B2B. LinkedIn only (and only in the footer). |
| Loading spinners that are not the standard thin circular type | Custom loading animations draw attention to waiting. |

### Visual effects — complete forbidden list

| Anti-pattern | Why it's forbidden |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Glassmorphism (frosted glass backgrounds) | Trend-driven. Distracts from the photography. |
| Neumorphism (soft UI shadows) | Makes UI look like plastic. Not industrial. |
| Gradient backgrounds of any kind | Flat colour only. |
| Blurred background blobs / orbs | Generic startup landing page visual. |
| Particle effects / floating dots | Distracting. No functional purpose. |
| Parallax scrolling | Unnecessary motion. Affects readability and performance. |
| Floating / hovering UI elements | Makes the interface feel detached from the page. |
| Full-screen overlays that cover content | Cookie banners, chat widgets, newsletter popups. Not on this site. |
| Animated page transitions that take >400ms | Users want to navigate, not watch a show. |
| Auto-playing video anywhere except the hero | Performance and distraction concern. |
| Video thumbnails with a "play" button overlay | The hero video plays automatically. No other video exists. |
| Horizontal scroll within any section | Breaks all responsive contracts. |

### Charts / data — complete forbidden list

| Anti-pattern | Why it's forbidden |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Pie charts, donut charts | Hard to read precisely. Can't show many categories. |
| 3D charts | Distorts data. Looks outdated. |
| Gauge / speedometer charts | Takes too much space for one data point. |
| Filled area charts | Area below the line adds no information. |
| Radar / spider charts | Hard to read. Unfamiliar format for B2B audience. |
| Charts with more than 2 colours | Use navy + muted only. |
| Charts with gridlines | Clean background. Gridlines add noise. |
| Charts with drop shadows | Flat data presentation. |
| Animated charts that take >2s to settle | Data should be immediately readable. |
| "Real-time" or live-updating numbers on a static page | Dishonest if not actually live. |
